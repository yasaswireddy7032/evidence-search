Harbor

A private place for women who have experienced sexual violence to say what they can't say out loud yet, and be met with belief instead of questions. It runs entirely on the phone.

Open the app ↗ Code, tests & design notes ↗ Download source ↗

Why I built it

A lot of survivors never tell anyone. Shame, self-blame and the fear of not being believed keep it inside. I wanted something for the moment before someone is ready to call a hotline: a place to type it, or just tap how you feel, and hear "I believe you" and "it wasn't your fault" without being asked why or for details.

How it works

The listener is a pure function on the device. Safety checks always run first: thoughts of self-harm bring up 988, immediate danger brings up 911 and the quick exit, and a very recent assault gets gentle information about time-sensitive medical care with no pressure to decide. Then it recognizes themes like freezing, self-blame, "it was someone I knew" and numbness, and replies briefly in a way that validates.

Around it: tap-to-say feelings, full-screen cards to show a nurse or counselor (which it can read aloud), a private journal with a "let it go" option, breathing and grounding, a no-pressure guide to options, real talks from survivors with content notes, a safety plan, and one-tap hotlines.

Privacy by design

No server, no account, no analytics. Nothing is saved unless she sets a passcode, and then it's PBKDF2 → AES-256-GCM on the device. There's a quick exit on every screen, navigation that leaves no back-button trail, a home-screen name of "Notes", and a strict Content Security Policy. An optional model can only be one running on her own device, and its replies are screened for blaming or doubting language before she sees them.

What I tested

14 tests cover crisis routing (self-harm, danger, recent assault, and false positives like "end it with him") and check that no reply asks "why", doubts her, or runs too long for someone who is overwhelmed. I also checked encryption in the browser: storage holds only ciphertext, a wrong passcode fails, and erasing removes everything.

Limits

It hasn't been reviewed by a rape crisis center yet, and that's the next step before anyone relies on it. US resources only, English only, and the listener is rule-based by default.

Example

her:    something happened with my boyfriend and i
        froze. i keep thinking it was my fault

harbor: Freezing is one of the most common ways a
        body responds to danger. It happens

        isn't consent, and it isn't a failure. It
        wasn't your fault. Not what you wore, not
        what you drank, not where you went, not that
        you knew them. The only person responsible
        is the one who chose to hurt you.
        [What consent means]

harbor: I believe you.
